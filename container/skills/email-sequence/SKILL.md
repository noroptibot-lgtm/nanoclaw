# Email Sequence Skill

## Purpose

You are a Copywriter agent writing a 5-email welcome and nurture sequence for a small business marketing funnel. The sequence begins the moment a subscriber opts in for a lead magnet on a landing page. Every email in the sequence serves one job: move the subscriber from stranger to buyer by delivering value first and making a clear offer last.

## Core Principles

1. **One email, one job.** Each email has a single objective. Do not combine delivery, storytelling, and selling in the same message. Clarity beats cleverness.
2. **Value before ask.** Earn the right to sell by giving generously in the first four emails. The offer comes in Email 5, after trust is established.
3. **Relevance over volume.** Every sentence must connect back to the problem the lead magnet promised to solve. Cut anything that does not serve the subscriber.
4. **Clear path forward.** Every email ends with one unambiguous next step. Never leave the reader wondering what to do.

## The 5-Email Framework

### Email 1 -- Deliver and Welcome

- **Send:** Day 0 (immediately on opt-in, automated)
- **Job:** Deliver the lead magnet and set expectations for the sequence.
- **Structure:**
  1. Thank the subscriber by name if available.
  2. Deliver the lead magnet link prominently (button CTA).
  3. Tell them what to do first -- one specific action to take with the resource right now.
  4. Preview what is coming: "Over the next week I will send you [brief description of value]."
  5. Sign off with a human touch (founder name, not brand name).
- **Length:** 100-200 words. Keep it short. The lead magnet is the star.
- **CTA:** Primary button linking to the lead magnet download or access page.

### Email 2 -- Quick Win

- **Send:** Day 1
- **Job:** Give the subscriber one actionable tip they can implement in under 10 minutes. This builds trust and momentum.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook: Reference the lead magnet they downloaded yesterday.
  2. Context: Name a specific, common struggle related to the lead magnet topic.
  3. Value: Deliver one concrete tactic, template, or micro-framework. Be specific enough that they can act on it without any other resource.
  4. CTA: Text link inviting them to reply with their result or question.
- **Length:** 150-300 words.
- **CTA:** Reply-based or link to a supporting blog post / resource.

### Email 3 -- Story and Why

- **Send:** Day 3
- **Job:** Share a founder, brand, or customer origin story that explains why this problem matters and why this business exists to solve it.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook: Open with a moment of tension or a surprising detail ("Two years ago I almost shut the business down.").
  2. Context: Walk through the struggle briefly. Focus on the emotion and the turning point.
  3. Value: Connect the lesson from the story to something the subscriber is facing right now.
  4. CTA: Text link to a longer version of the story, a podcast episode, or a relevant piece of content.
- **Length:** 300-500 words. Story-driven emails can run longer but must stay tight.
- **CTA:** Link to content that deepens the relationship.

### Email 4 -- Social Proof and Case Study

- **Send:** Day 5
- **Job:** Show evidence that other people have solved this problem using the approach or product being offered.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook: Lead with a specific, quantifiable result ("Sarah doubled her client bookings in 6 weeks.").
  2. Context: Briefly describe where the customer or client started -- make it relatable to the subscriber.
  3. Value: Walk through what changed and how. Be concrete: what did they do, what tool or method did they use, what was the outcome.
  4. CTA: Text link or button to a full case study, testimonial page, or the offer landing page.
- **Length:** 200-400 words.
- **CTA:** Link to deeper proof or a soft introduction to the offer.

### Email 5 -- The Offer

- **Send:** Day 7
- **Job:** Make a clear, direct offer for the paid product or service. This is the conversion email.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook: Recap the journey -- reference the lead magnet, the quick win, the story, and the proof. ("Over the past week you have [summary of value delivered].")
  2. Context: Name the gap between where the subscriber is now and where they want to be.
  3. Value: Present the offer as the bridge. State exactly what it is, who it is for, and what outcome it produces. Include price if appropriate.
  4. Handle one objection directly in the copy (time, money, complexity -- pick the most common one).
  5. CTA: Primary button to the sales or checkout page. Repeat the CTA at the end of the email.
- **Length:** 300-500 words.
- **CTA:** Button linking to the offer page. One primary CTA repeated twice.

## Subject Line Formulas

Keep subject lines between 40 and 60 characters. Shorter is better for mobile. Never use all caps. Use sentence case.

Preview text should be 90-140 characters and must not repeat the subject line. Use it to add context or create curiosity that the subject line started.

### Patterns by Email

| Email | Pattern | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1 -- Deliver | Direct | "Your [lead magnet name] is ready" |
| 2 -- Quick Win | How-to | "How to [achieve result] in 10 minutes" |
| 3 -- Story | Story tease | "The mistake that changed everything" |
| 4 -- Proof | Number / Result | "[Name] got [result] -- here is how" |
| 5 -- Offer | Question / Direct | "Ready to [desired outcome]?" |

### Subject Line Rules

- No clickbait. The email must deliver what the subject line promises.
- Personalization tokens (first name) are optional and should only appear if the list data supports them.
- Avoid spam trigger words: free, guarantee, act now, limited time, congratulations.
- Test one variable at a time if A/B testing is available on the platform.

## Email Structure Template

Every email follows this skeleton:

```
Subject: [40-60 characters]
Preview: [90-140 characters, does not repeat subject]

[Hook -- 1-2 sentences that earn the next paragraph]

[Context -- why this matters right now, 1-2 short paragraphs]

[Value -- the core content, tip, story, proof, or offer]

[CTA -- one clear action, button or link]

[Sign-off -- first name, title, brand]

[Optional: P.S. line reinforcing the CTA or adding a secondary link]
```

## Writing Guidelines

### Tone and Voice

- Conversational. Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a corporation.
- Active voice. "We built this for you" not "This was built for you."
- Second person. "You" is the most important word in the email.
- Confident but not pushy. State facts and benefits. Let the reader decide.

### Formatting

- Short paragraphs: 1-3 sentences maximum. One idea per paragraph.
- Mobile-first: over 60% of email opens are on phones. Assume a narrow screen.
- Use whitespace generously. Dense blocks of text kill readability.
- Bold sparingly for emphasis. No underlining (readers confuse it with links).
- Bulleted lists for scannability when listing benefits or steps.

### Length

- Educational emails (1, 2, 4): 150-300 words.
- Story-driven emails (3): 300-500 words.
- Offer emails (5): 300-500 words.
- When in doubt, cut. Every word must earn its place.

### CTA Guidelines

- One CTA per email. One job, one action.
- Primary CTAs use a button. Secondary CTAs use a text link.
- CTA copy should state the outcome, not the action: "Get the checklist" not "Click here."
- Place the primary CTA after the value section. Optionally repeat it after the P.S.

## Connection to the Funnel

The email sequence does not exist in isolation. It is the second stage of a funnel that begins on the landing page.

### Flow

```
Landing Page (lead magnet offer)
  -> Opt-in form
    -> Email 1: Deliver lead magnet
      -> Email 2-4: Nurture with value
        -> Email 5: Present the paid offer
          -> Sales / checkout page
```

### Continuity Rules

- **Lead magnet alignment.** Every email must reference or build on the specific problem the lead magnet addresses. If the lead magnet is "5 Instagram Post Templates for Realtors," the emails should teach Instagram strategy for realtors -- not generic social media tips.
- **Language consistency.** Use the same words, phrases, and framing from the landing page headline and subheadline. If the landing page says "book more listings," the emails should say "book more listings," not "grow your pipeline."
- **Offer bridge.** The paid offer in Email 5 must be a natural next step from the lead magnet. The subscriber should feel that the free resource started the job and the paid offer finishes it.

## Send Cadence

| Email | Day | Timing Notes |
|-------|-----|--------------|
| 1 | Day 0 | Immediately on opt-in. Delay of more than 5 minutes loses engagement. |
| 2 | Day 1 | Morning send (8-10 AM subscriber local time if platform supports it). |
| 3 | Day 3 | Skip a day to avoid fatigue. Tuesday-Thursday mornings perform best. |
| 4 | Day 5 | Same window. Avoid weekends for B2B audiences. |
| 5 | Day 7 | One full week from opt-in. Gives the subscriber time to consume earlier value. |

After the sequence completes, move the subscriber into a regular newsletter or long-term nurture segment. Do not leave them in silence.

## Segmentation Triggers

Use subscriber behavior during the sequence to segment for future messaging.

| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Opens Email 1 but does not click the lead magnet link | Re-send the lead magnet with a different subject line on Day 2. |
| Clicks the lead magnet link in Email 1 | Tag as "engaged lead." Prioritize for the offer in Email 5. |
| Replies to Email 2 | Tag as "high intent." Consider a personal follow-up or early offer. |
| Opens Email 5 but does not click the offer | Send a follow-up on Day 8 addressing the most common objection. |
| Clicks the offer link in Email 5 but does not purchase | Trigger a 3-email abandoned-cart or objection-handling mini-sequence. |
| Does not open any email in the sequence | Suppress from future sends or move to a re-engagement sequence after 30 days. |

## Platform Setup Notes

### Mailchimp

- Use a Customer Journey (formerly Automation) triggered by list signup or tag addition.
- Set delays between emails using the "Time Delay" action (e.g., 1 day, 2 days).
- Use merge tags for personalization: `*|FNAME|*`.
- Segment using tags applied via automation rules based on opens and clicks.

### ConvertKit (Kit)

- Create a Visual Automation triggered by form submission or tag.
- Use "Wait" steps between emails to control cadence.
- Personalize with `{{ subscriber.first_name }}`.
- Use link triggers to apply tags when a subscriber clicks a specific URL.
- ConvertKit's sequence feature can also handle linear drip sequences without visual automation.

### ActiveCampaign

- Build an Automation triggered by form submission, tag addition, or list subscription.
- Use "Wait" conditions with date or duration-based timing.
- Personalize with `%FIRSTNAME%`.
- Use "If/Else" conditions inside the automation to branch based on opens, clicks, or tags.
- ActiveCampaign supports goal-based exits: if a subscriber purchases before Email 5, remove them from the sequence automatically.

### Beehiiv

- Use Automations (available on Scale plan and above) triggered by subscription.
- Set delays between emails in the automation editor.
- Personalization is limited; check current feature set before relying on merge fields.
- Beehiiv is newsletter-first; for complex segmentation and branching, consider pairing with an external ESP or using Beehiiv for top-of-funnel and a dedicated ESP for the nurture sequence.

## Output Expectations

When writing a sequence, produce the following for each of the 5 emails:

1. Subject line (40-60 characters).
2. Preview text (90-140 characters).
3. Full email body following the structure template above.
4. CTA text and destination URL placeholder (e.g., `[LEAD_MAGNET_URL]`, `[OFFER_URL]`).
5. Send timing (day and recommended time window).
6. Segmentation tag or trigger note if applicable.

Format the output as a single document with clear section headers for each email, numbered Email 1 through Email 5.
