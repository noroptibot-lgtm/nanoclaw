# Strategy Outreach Skill

This skill provides the methodology for writing outreach that opens doors using content strategy deliverables as proof of work. The key insight: showing someone you already built something for them is 10x more effective than telling them you could.

## Core Principle

Every outreach piece follows the same pattern:
1. **I noticed something about your business** (shows research)
2. **There's a gap / opportunity** (creates urgency)
3. **I went ahead and built something** (proves capability)
4. **Here's what it looks like** (concrete deliverables)
5. **Want to talk about implementing it?** (soft CTA)

## Cover Letter / Cold Email Framework

### The Strategy Cold Outreach Structure

**Subject Lines** (3 options per email):
1. **Proof hook**: References what you built ("I built a 90-day content strategy for [Company]")
2. **Gap hook**: References the opportunity ("Your content is good. Your strategy could be great.")
3. **Curiosity hook**: Creates intrigue ("Something I noticed about [Company]'s content")

**Email Body** (150-250 words):

**Opening (1-2 sentences)**:
- Reference their specific content or business -- show you did your homework
- Mention something specific (their latest post, their website, their social presence)
- NO flattery filler ("I was so impressed by your incredible...")

**The Gap (2-3 sentences)**:
- Identify the strategic opportunity: great business, inconsistent (or missing) content strategy
- Frame as opportunity, not criticism: "Your [product/service] deserves a content engine that matches its quality"
- Be specific: "Your LinkedIn has 3 posts this month; your competitor has 15 and is ranking for your keywords"

**What I Built (1-2 sentences)**:
- "I went ahead and built a content strategy for [Company]"
- Reference specific deliverables: brand voice document, 30/90-day calendar, platform templates
- For light mode: mention brand voice doc + 30-day calendar
- For full mode: mention the complete package

**The CTA (1-2 sentences)**:
- One soft ask: suggest a call to walk through the strategy
- Include member contact info
- No pricing, no pressure

### Writing Rules for Cover Letters
- **First person** from the member's perspective
- **Conversational, not corporate** -- match the member's niche tone
- **Specific, not generic** -- reference THIS prospect's actual business/content
- **No pricing** -- the email opens the door; pricing comes in conversation
- **No flattery filler** -- no "hope this finds you well", no "I'm a huge fan"
- **150-250 words** -- short enough to actually get read
- **Single gap focus** -- the gap is always "content strategy opportunity"
- **3 subject lines** -- always provide 3 options
- **Standalone file** -- the cover letter is complete on its own

### Light Mode vs Full Mode Email Differences

**Light mode cold email**:
- Say "I analyzed your brand and mapped out a content plan" NOT "I built a complete strategy package"
- Reference the 2 deliverables: brand voice document + 30-day calendar
- CTA: "This is what I do for clients -- want to see what the full 90-day strategy looks like?"
- Do NOT mention the full package, templates, outreach sequences, etc.

**Full mode cover letter**:
- Reference the complete strategy package with specific deliverables
- Mention the number of templates, the calendar scope, the competitor analysis
- CTA: "Want to walk through the strategy and see how to implement it?"

## DM Script Framework

### The Value-First DM

Short (3-5 sentences), specific, and value-forward:

```
Hey [Name] -- I saw your [specific post/content] about [topic]. Really smart take on [specific point].

I noticed [specific observation about their content/strategy]. [One-sentence opportunity or insight].

I actually put together a [brief description of what you built] -- would you want me to send it over?
```

### DM Script Variations

**The Compliment + Insight DM**:
- Genuine compliment about specific content
- Share one strategic insight they might not have considered
- Offer to share more

**The Competitor Intel DM**:
- Note something their competitor is doing well on content
- Frame as an opportunity, not a threat
- Offer your analysis

**The Quick Win DM**:
- Identify one easy content improvement they could make today
- Give it away for free (builds trust)
- Mention you do this professionally

### Platform-Specific DM Notes

**LinkedIn DMs**: Professional tone, can be slightly longer (5-7 sentences). Connection request message limited to 300 characters.

**Instagram DMs**: Casual tone, very short (2-4 sentences). Must feel natural, not like a sales pitch. Best sent in response to a Story.

**X DMs**: Brief, direct. Often best as a follow-up to a public reply. Can reference a thread or tweet.

## Partnership Pitch Framework

### Structure (250-400 words)

1. **Mutual connection**: How you found them, any shared connections
2. **Mutual value**: What you bring to them AND what they bring to you
3. **Specific idea**: One concrete collaboration idea (not vague "let's work together")
4. **Social proof**: Your audience size, past collaborations, or relevant results
5. **Clear next step**: Specific ask (15-min call, DM exchange, email thread)

### Partnership Pitch Types

**Content Swap**: "You guest post on my blog, I guest post on yours"
**Audience Swap**: "You mention me to your email list, I mention you to mine"
**Co-Creation**: "We create a joint guide/webinar/podcast episode"
**Cross-Promotion**: "We share each other's best content for a week"

## Collaboration Proposal Framework

### Structure (formal, 400-600 words)

1. **Introduction**: Who you are and why you're reaching out
2. **The Opportunity**: What the collaboration could achieve
3. **The Proposal**: Specific deliverables, timeline, and roles
4. **Audience Overlap**: Data on how your audiences complement each other
5. **Past Results**: Case studies or examples of previous collaborations
6. **Next Steps**: Clear timeline and decision process

### Quality Standards for All Outreach

1. **Specific**: References the recipient's actual content/business (not templated)
2. **Value-first**: Gives something before asking for anything
3. **Concise**: Respects the recipient's time
4. **No pressure**: Soft CTA, no urgency manufacturing
5. **Professional**: No typos, no over-familiarity, no desperation
6. **Credible**: Includes relevant social proof without bragging
7. **Actionable**: Clear, specific next step
