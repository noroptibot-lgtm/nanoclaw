# Brand Voice Skill

This skill provides the methodology for extracting or constructing a brand's voice and creating a client-facing brand voice document. Voice consistency is the #1 quality signal for content strategy deliverables -- if the calendar, templates, and outreach don't sound like the client, the strategy fails.

## Core Principle

A brand voice is not just tone. It's the intersection of the brand's values, their audience's expectations, and the personality that emerges when those two meet. A good brand voice document should let any writer produce content that sounds like the brand without needing to talk to anyone at the company.

## The Voice Discovery Framework

### Source 1: Existing Content Analysis

When the client has existing content (website, social media, blog, email), extract their voice from it:

**Tone Spectrum** -- Rate across five axes:

| Dimension | Left | Center | Right |
|-----------|------|--------|-------|
| Formality | Casual slang, abbreviations | Conversational, natural | Polished, precise |
| Energy | Calm, measured, reflective | Engaged, steady | Intense, urgent, passionate |
| Warmth | Clinical, detached | Friendly, approachable | Intimate, personal |
| Authority | Peer-level, exploratory | Confident guide | Definitive expert |
| Humor | Deadpan serious | Occasional wit, dry humor | Playful, comedic |

**Vocabulary Fingerprint**:
- Signature phrases: Recurring expressions unique to the brand
- Industry jargon: Technical terms used fluently (and which ones are avoided)
- Casual markers: Contractions, filler words, slang
- Intensifiers: How they emphasize ("very", "incredibly", "insanely", or none)
- Avoid list: Words that feel off-brand (corporate jargon for casual brands, slang for professional brands)

**Sentence Architecture**:
- Length distribution: Short and punchy? Mixed? Long and flowing?
- Opening patterns: Questions? Bold statements? "Here's the thing..."?
- Punctuation style: Em dashes? Ellipses? Exclamation marks?
- Paragraph density: One-liners? Dense paragraphs?

### Source 2: Business Values Construction

When the client has minimal existing content, construct their voice from:

1. **Business values**: What do they stand for? What would they never do?
2. **Target audience**: Who are they talking to? What language does that audience use?
3. **Industry norms**: What does their industry typically sound like? Should they match or contrast?
4. **Competitive positioning**: How do competitors sound? Where's the white space?
5. **Founder personality**: How does the founder/owner naturally communicate?

### Source 3: Brand Guidelines (if provided)

Existing brand guidelines are a starting point, not gospel. Check for:
- Stated values vs. actual content voice (they often don't match)
- Outdated tone guidance (written 5 years ago, never updated)
- Generic language ("professional yet approachable" -- means nothing without examples)

## The Brand Voice Document Structure

The client-facing brand voice document is a polished deliverable, not a raw analysis. Structure it for clarity and usability:

### Section 1: Voice Summary
- Three-word voice description (e.g., "Bold, Warm, Direct")
- One-paragraph voice overview
- "This brand sounds like..." comparison (e.g., "A knowledgeable friend who happens to be an expert")

### Section 2: Tone Spectrum
- Visual spectrum for each dimension with the brand's position marked
- Examples from existing content (or constructed examples if new brand)
- Context shifts: How the tone adjusts for different situations (crisis, celebration, education)

### Section 3: Vocabulary Guide
- Words to use: With examples in context
- Words to avoid: With explanations why
- Signature phrases: Brand-specific language to use consistently
- Industry terms: How to handle jargon (use it? explain it? avoid it?)

### Section 4: Platform Voice Adaptation
For each platform the strategy targets:
- What stays the same (core voice)
- What amplifies (platform-rewarded traits)
- What adjusts (format-driven changes)
- Example post in the brand's voice

### Section 5: Voice Dos and Don'ts
- Specific, actionable guidelines with before/after examples
- "Write this, not that" format
- Common pitfalls for this specific brand

### Section 6: Voice Test
- 3-5 sample sentences showing how the brand would naturally phrase common content types:
  - A product announcement
  - A customer success story
  - A response to criticism
  - A casual social post
  - A sales pitch

## Platform-Specific Voice Adaptation

### X/Twitter Voice
- Amplify: Confidence, directness, personality
- Compress: Nuance, qualifiers, complex arguments
- Add: Hooks, bold claims, thread-native storytelling
- Drop: Formal transitions, lengthy explanations

### LinkedIn Voice
- Amplify: Authority, professional credibility, thought leadership
- Maintain: Core personality, first-person perspective
- Adjust: Slightly more professional, less casual
- Drop: Excessive slang, overly casual references

### Instagram Voice
- Amplify: Warmth, visual thinking, accessibility
- Simplify: Complex ideas into bite-sized insights
- Add: Emotional resonance, motivational energy
- Drop: Dense arguments, heavy data

### Email Voice
- Amplify: Intimacy, personal connection, exclusivity
- Maintain: Core vocabulary and sentence patterns
- Add: Direct reader address, conversational asides
- Drop: Public performance elements, platform-specific hooks

### Blog Voice
- Amplify: Depth, expertise, thoroughness
- Maintain: Core voice through longer format
- Add: SEO structure, internal links, comprehensive coverage
- Watch for: Corporate drift in second half of pieces

## The Questionnaire Fallback

For businesses with truly minimal content, use this structured questionnaire to construct a voice:

1. **If your brand were a person at a party, how would they act?** (Wallflower? Life of the party? The one having deep 1-on-1 conversations?)
2. **What 3 brands do you admire?** (Not competitors -- brands whose communication style you respect)
3. **Finish this sentence: "Our customers describe us as ___"**
4. **What's one thing you'd NEVER say to a customer?**
5. **How do you sign off emails to customers?** (Formal? Casual? Emoji?)
6. **Do you use humor? If so, what kind?** (Dry wit? Puns? Self-deprecating? None?)
7. **What industry jargon do your customers actually use?**
8. **What's the #1 emotion you want customers to feel after reading your content?**

## Voice Consistency Verification

After building the voice profile or brand voice document, verify:

1. **The uniqueness test**: Could this voice document apply to any business in the industry? If yes, it's too generic.
2. **The recognition test**: If someone read a post written with this voice guide, could they guess the brand? If no, it needs more specificity.
3. **The range test**: Does the document cover how voice adapts across situations (casual vs. serious, happy vs. crisis)?
4. **The practical test**: Could a new content writer produce on-brand content using only this document?
5. **The avoid test**: Are the "don't" guidelines as specific and useful as the "do" guidelines?

## Common Voice Document Failures

1. **Too generic**: "Professional yet approachable" describes every brand. Add examples, signature phrases, and specific vocabulary.
2. **No avoid list**: Knowing what NOT to say is as important as knowing what to say.
3. **Missing platform adaptation**: A voice guide without platform-specific guidance is incomplete.
4. **No examples**: Every guideline should include a before/after or "write this, not that" example.
5. **Values =/= voice**: "We value innovation" doesn't tell a writer how to sound. "We use tech-forward language, avoid buzzwords, and explain complex ideas with everyday analogies" does.
