# Copywriting Skill

This skill contains frameworks, formulas, and principles for writing conversion-focused copy across every stage of a small business marketing funnel. Use it to produce landing pages, lead magnets, email sequences, thank-you pages, and ad copy that sound like the client -- not like a marketing textbook.


## Core Principles

These principles govern all copy you produce. They are non-negotiable.

### Clarity Over Cleverness

Say what you mean. A confused reader never converts. If a sentence requires a second read to understand, rewrite it. Puns, wordplay, and insider references belong nowhere near a headline or CTA. The reader should know within five seconds what the page offers, who it is for, and what to do next.

### Benefits Over Features

Features describe what something is. Benefits describe what it does for the reader. Always lead with benefits. Features can appear as supporting evidence, but never as the headline or hook.

- Feature: "24/7 live chat support"
- Benefit: "Get answers the moment you need them -- day or night"
- Feature: "AI-powered scheduling"
- Benefit: "Stop wasting 3 hours a week on back-and-forth booking emails"

### Specificity Over Vagueness

Vague copy feels like filler. Specific copy feels like proof. Replace generalities with numbers, timeframes, names, and concrete outcomes whenever possible.

- Vague: "We help businesses grow"
- Specific: "We helped 137 local restaurants increase weeknight reservations by 23% in 90 days"
- Vague: "Save time on your workflow"
- Specific: "Cut your invoice processing from 45 minutes to 6 minutes per batch"

### Customer Language Over Company Language

Write the way the customer talks about their problem, not the way the business talks about its solution. Pull exact phrases from reviews, testimonials, intake forms, and support tickets. If a plumber's customers say "my basement floods every spring," do not write "seasonal water intrusion mitigation." Write "stop your basement from flooding this spring."

### One Idea Per Section

Each section of a page, each email, each ad -- one idea. One promise. One call to action. Stacking multiple messages dilutes all of them. If you have three benefits, give each its own block with its own supporting evidence.

### Simple Over Complex

Short sentences. Common words. Reading level of a smart eighth grader. This is not about dumbing things down. It is about removing friction between the reader and the message.

- Complex: "Our proprietary methodology leverages synergistic frameworks to optimize your operational throughput"
- Simple: "We help you get more done in less time using a system that actually works"

### Active Over Passive Voice

Active voice is direct, energetic, and clear. Passive voice is weak and evasive.

- Passive: "Your results will be delivered within 48 hours"
- Active: "You get your results within 48 hours"
- Passive: "A custom plan is created for each client"
- Active: "We build a custom plan for you"

### Show Over Tell

Do not tell the reader something is "amazing" or "revolutionary." Show them the outcome with evidence: a number, a story, a before-and-after, a testimonial. Adjectives are not proof.

- Telling: "Our amazing coaching program transforms lives"
- Showing: "After 12 weeks, Sarah went from dreading Monday mornings to launching a business that replaced her salary"


## Persuasion Frameworks

Use these frameworks to structure copy at every level -- full pages, individual sections, emails, and ads. Most copy should use one primary framework with elements of others woven in.

### AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

The classic four-stage progression. Works well for landing pages and longer-form copy.

1. **Attention**: Open with something that stops the scroll. A bold claim, a surprising statistic, a question that hits a nerve.
2. **Interest**: Expand on the problem or opportunity. Make the reader see themselves in the story.
3. **Desire**: Present the solution and paint the outcome. Use specifics, social proof, and future-pacing.
4. **Action**: Tell them exactly what to do next. One clear CTA.

### PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)

The workhorse framework. Short, punchy, effective. Ideal for ads, emails, and above-the-fold landing page sections.

1. **Problem**: Name the pain the reader is experiencing. Use their language.
2. **Agitate**: Make the pain vivid. What happens if they do nothing? What is the cost of inaction? Do not invent pain -- amplify what is already real.
3. **Solution**: Present the offer as the resolution. Bridge directly from the agitation to relief.

### Before-After-Bridge

Good for testimonial-driven sections and transformation-focused copy.

1. **Before**: Describe the reader's current situation. Make it specific and recognizable.
2. **After**: Describe the outcome they want. Make it vivid and desirable.
3. **Bridge**: Show how the product or service gets them from before to after.

### PASTOR (Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response)

A longer-form framework suited to sales pages and high-ticket landing pages.

1. **Problem**: Identify the core pain.
2. **Amplify**: Deepen the emotional stakes. What does this problem cost them in time, money, stress, relationships?
3. **Story**: Tell a relatable story -- the client's story, a customer's story, or a hypothetical that mirrors the reader's experience.
4. **Transformation**: Show the before-and-after. Concrete results.
5. **Offer**: Present what they get. Stack the value.
6. **Response**: Ask for the action. Overcome the final objection.

### 4 Ps (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push)

Effective for mid-length copy blocks and email bodies.

1. **Promise**: Make a clear, specific promise relevant to the reader's goal.
2. **Picture**: Help them visualize what life looks like after the promise is fulfilled.
3. **Proof**: Back it up with evidence -- testimonials, data, case studies, credentials.
4. **Push**: Create urgency or give a reason to act now.

### Star-Story-Solution

Narrative-driven framework. Works well in email sequences and founder-story sections.

1. **Star**: Introduce the main character (often the customer or the founder).
2. **Story**: Walk through their struggle. Build empathy.
3. **Solution**: Show how they found the answer. Connect it to the offer.


## Headline Formulas

Headlines do one job: get the next line read. Every headline should pass the "so what" test. If a stranger could read it and shrug, it is not specific enough.

### Result-Based Headlines

1. **How to [Achieve Desired Result] Without [Common Objection]**
   - "How to Fill Your Appointment Calendar Without Spending a Dime on Ads"

2. **[Number] Ways to [Achieve Desired Result] in [Timeframe]**
   - "7 Ways to Double Your Catering Orders in 90 Days"

3. **The [Timeframe] Plan to [Desired Result]**
   - "The 30-Day Plan to a Fully Booked Salon"

4. **Get [Desired Result] Even If [Objection/Limitation]**
   - "Get 5 New Clients This Month Even If You Hate Selling"

5. **[Desired Result] in [Timeframe] -- Guaranteed**
   - "A Cleaner Home in 2 Hours -- Guaranteed"

### Problem-Based Headlines

6. **Tired of [Frustrating Problem]?**
   - "Tired of Spending Your Weekends on Bookkeeping?"

7. **Stop [Painful Activity] and Start [Desired Activity]**
   - "Stop Chasing Leads and Start Closing Deals"

8. **Why [Common Approach] Is Costing You [What They Lose]**
   - "Why Word-of-Mouth Alone Is Costing You $4,000 a Month"

9. **The #1 Mistake [Audience] Makes With [Topic]**
   - "The #1 Mistake New Gym Owners Make With Their Pricing"

10. **[Painful Problem]? Here Is What to Do Instead**
    - "Clients Ghosting After the First Call? Here Is What to Do Instead"

### Curiosity-Based Headlines

11. **What [Successful Group] Knows About [Topic] That You Don't**
    - "What Top-Earning Freelancers Know About Pricing That You Don't"

12. **The Secret to [Desired Result] That [Experts/Competitors] Won't Tell You**
    - "The Secret to Ranking on Google That Most SEO Agencies Won't Tell You"

13. **[Surprising Fact or Number] About [Topic]**
    - "83% of Local Businesses Ignore the One Marketing Channel That Actually Works"

14. **We Analyzed [Number] [Things] -- Here Is What We Found**
    - "We Analyzed 500 Local Ad Campaigns -- Here Is What the Winners Had in Common"

### Social Proof Headlines

15. **How [Client Name/Type] [Achieved Result] in [Timeframe]**
    - "How a One-Person Law Firm Signed 14 New Clients in 60 Days"

16. **[Number] [Audience Members] Can't Be Wrong**
    - "2,300 Small Business Owners Can't Be Wrong"

17. **Join [Number] [Audience] Who Already [Desired Result]**
    - "Join 800+ Coaches Who Already Fill Their Programs Without Social Media"

### Direct Offer Headlines

18. **[Free/Discounted] [Thing] for [Specific Audience]**
    - "Free Marketing Audit for Local Service Businesses"

19. **Finally, a [Product/Service] Built for [Specific Audience]**
    - "Finally, a CRM Built for Contractors Who Hate Software"

20. **[Do Desired Thing] -- Without [Pain Point]. Here Is How.**
    - "Book 10+ Appointments a Week -- Without Cold Calling. Here Is How."

### Question Headlines

21. **What Would You Do With [Desirable Outcome]?**
    - "What Would You Do With 15 Extra Hours Every Week?"

22. **Are You Making These [Number] [Topic] Mistakes?**
    - "Are You Making These 3 Costly Hiring Mistakes?"

23. **What If You Could [Desired Result] in Just [Timeframe]?**
    - "What If You Could Launch Your Online Course in Just 2 Weeks?"

### Contrast Headlines

24. **[Small Input], [Big Result]**
    - "One Email Sequence, 47 New Customers"

25. **[Old Way] vs. [New Way]: Why [Audience] Are Switching**
    - "Flyers vs. Facebook Ads: Why Local Gyms Are Switching"


## Lead Magnet Title Formulas

Lead magnet titles must promise a specific, valuable outcome and signal low effort to consume. The title is doing the same job as a headline -- it must pass the "would I actually download this?" test.

### Checklist / Cheat Sheet Titles

1. **The [Topic] Checklist: [Number] Steps to [Desired Result]**
   - "The Grand Opening Checklist: 27 Steps to a Packed First Week"

2. **The [Audience]'s Cheat Sheet to [Desired Result]**
   - "The Freelancer's Cheat Sheet to Raising Your Rates Without Losing Clients"

3. **[Number] Things to [Do/Check/Fix] Before You [Action]**
   - "11 Things to Fix on Your Website Before You Spend Another Dollar on Ads"

### Guide / Blueprint Titles

4. **The [Audience] Blueprint for [Desired Result]**
   - "The Solo Consultant Blueprint for Landing $10K Clients"

5. **The Ultimate Guide to [Desired Result] for [Audience]**
   - "The Ultimate Guide to Google Reviews for Local Restaurants"

6. **[Desired Result] in [Timeframe]: A Step-by-Step Guide for [Audience]**
   - "Your First 1,000 Email Subscribers in 90 Days: A Step-by-Step Guide for Coaches"

### Template / Swipe File Titles

7. **[Number] Proven [Templates/Scripts/Emails] for [Desired Result]**
   - "5 Proven Follow-Up Emails That Turn Estimates Into Signed Contracts"

8. **Steal My [Thing]: The Exact [Asset] I Used to [Result]**
   - "Steal My Welcome Sequence: The Exact 5 Emails I Used to Onboard 300 Clients"

9. **The [Topic] Swipe File: [Number] Ready-to-Use [Assets]**
   - "The Social Media Swipe File: 30 Ready-to-Use Post Templates for Realtors"

### Mistake / Myth Titles

10. **[Number] [Topic] Mistakes That Are Costing [Audience] [What They Lose]**
    - "7 Pricing Mistakes That Are Costing Freelancers Thousands Every Year"

11. **[Number] [Topic] Myths [Audience] Still Believe (And What to Do Instead)**
    - "5 SEO Myths Small Business Owners Still Believe (And What to Do Instead)"

### Quiz / Assessment Titles

12. **Is Your [Thing] Ready for [Desired Outcome]? Take the [Number]-Point Assessment**
    - "Is Your Website Ready to Convert? Take the 10-Point Assessment"

13. **What Is Your [Topic] Score? Find Out in [Timeframe]**
    - "What Is Your Marketing Readiness Score? Find Out in 3 Minutes"


## CTA (Call to Action) Copy

### Principles

Every page, email, and ad gets one primary CTA. The CTA tells the reader exactly what to do and what they get when they do it.

Weak CTAs are vague and self-serving:
- "Submit"
- "Click Here"
- "Learn More"
- "Contact Us"

Strong CTAs are specific and benefit-focused. They follow this formula:

**Action Verb + What They Get**

Examples:
- "Download Your Free Checklist"
- "Book Your Free 15-Minute Audit"
- "Get My Custom Growth Plan"
- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Send Me the Templates"
- "Reserve My Spot"
- "Show Me the Pricing"
- "Unlock My Discount"

### CTA Button Copy Rules

1. Write from the reader's perspective. "Start My Free Trial" not "Start Your Free Trial" (first person outperforms second person on buttons).
2. Specificity wins. "Get My 5-Email Sequence" beats "Download Now."
3. Reduce perceived risk. Add "Free," "No credit card required," or "Cancel anytime" near the button.
4. One CTA per section. If the page has multiple sections, each section can repeat the same CTA. Do not offer competing actions.
5. Supporting text below the button should handle the final objection: "No spam. Unsubscribe anytime." / "Takes less than 60 seconds." / "Join 2,000+ business owners."


## Voice and Tone

### Establishing the Client's Voice

You are writing as the client's business, not as yourself. Every piece of copy should sound like the business owner talking to their ideal customer. To calibrate voice, answer these questions before writing:

1. **Formality level**: Is this business casual and conversational, or polished and professional? A neighborhood plumber and a wealth management firm do not sound the same.
2. **Vocabulary level**: Does the audience use industry jargon, or do they need plain language? A B2B SaaS company selling to developers can say "API endpoint." A bakery selling to families should not say "SKU rationalization."
3. **Personality markers**: Is the brand funny, warm, authoritative, rebellious, nurturing, no-nonsense? Pick two or three adjectives and write to them.
4. **Sentence rhythm**: Short and punchy (energy, urgency) or flowing and descriptive (warmth, storytelling)?
5. **Pronoun usage**: "We" (team-oriented), "I" (personal brand), or invisible (brand-as-institution)?

### Voice Calibration Process

Before writing any funnel copy, extract or infer the following from the client brief, existing website, reviews, and intake materials:

- Three adjectives that describe how the brand should sound (e.g., "warm, direct, confident")
- Two or three phrases the client or their customers use repeatedly
- One phrase the client would never say
- The reading level of the target audience (approximate)

Then write a single test paragraph -- the opening of the landing page -- and check it against those markers before continuing.

### Persona-Specific Adjustments

**Solo service provider (plumber, trainer, therapist):** First person. Conversational. Short sentences. "I have been doing this for 12 years" not "With over a decade of industry experience." Readers want to feel like they are hiring a person, not a corporation.

**Local business with a team (restaurant, dental office, gym):** "We" voice. Friendly but professional. Community references. "We have been part of the Eastside neighborhood since 2015" builds local trust.

**Online coach or consultant:** First person. Energetic. Story-driven. Slightly aspirational. "I went from burned out and broke to booked solid in six months -- and I will show you exactly how."

**Professional services (law firm, accounting, financial planning):** "We" or firm name. Measured. Authoritative. Fewer exclamation points. Trust and competence matter more than relatability. "Our clients sleep better knowing their taxes are handled by a team that has seen it all."

**E-commerce / product business:** Brand voice varies widely, but defaults to direct and benefit-focused. "You" oriented. "Your mornings are about to get a lot easier."


## Objection Handling

Small business prospects share a predictable set of objections. Address these proactively in copy rather than hoping the reader will not think of them.

### Price Objections

**"It's too expensive."**
- Reframe cost as investment: "For less than what you spend on coffee each month, you get a system that brings in 5 new clients."
- Compare to cost of inaction: "Every month without a working funnel, you leave $3,000-$5,000 on the table."
- Break the price down: "$97/month works out to $3.23/day."
- Show ROI: "Our average client makes back the cost of the program within 3 weeks."

**"I can do this myself / find it free online."**
- Acknowledge and redirect: "You can find pieces of this scattered across YouTube and blogs. What you cannot find is someone who has assembled it into a step-by-step system tested on 200+ businesses like yours."
- Emphasize time cost: "You could figure this out on your own. It will take 6-12 months and a lot of trial and error. Or you could have it running in two weeks."

### Trust Objections

**"How do I know this works?"**
- Social proof: Testimonials, case studies, number of customers served.
- Risk reversal: Money-back guarantees, free trials, "pay only if you see results."
- Specificity: Vague claims breed skepticism. "We increased bookings by 34% for a Denver-based massage therapist" is more believable than "we get great results."

**"I've been burned before by [marketers / agencies / coaches]."**
- Validate the skepticism: "If you have worked with a marketing agency that took your money and disappeared, we get it. That is exactly why we [specific differentiator: show you the dashboard, do monthly check-ins, offer a 90-day guarantee]."
- Differentiate: Name the bad practice and explain how you do it differently.

### Timing Objections

**"I'm not ready yet / now isn't the right time."**
- Future-pace the cost of waiting: "Where will your business be in 6 months if nothing changes?"
- Lower the commitment: "Start with the free checklist. No commitment required."
- Create urgency (only if real): Limited spots, enrollment windows, genuine deadlines.

**"I need to think about it."**
- Summarize the value: "Here is what you are deciding between: keep doing what you are doing, or [specific benefit] starting this week."
- Reduce friction: "You can cancel anytime. There is zero risk."

### Relevance Objections

**"This won't work for my industry / situation."**
- Use niche-specific proof: "We have done this for plumbers, dentists, yoga studios, and SaaS companies. Here is how we adapted it for [their industry]."
- Acknowledge uniqueness, then generalize: "Every business is different. But the principles of getting found online, building trust, and following up -- those are universal."

**"My business is too small for this."**
- Reframe: "This is built specifically for businesses your size. You do not need a big team or a big budget. You need the right system."


## Funnel Copy Architecture

A funnel is not a collection of disconnected pages. It is a single conversation spread across multiple touchpoints. Every piece of copy must connect to what came before and set up what comes next.

### The Funnel as a Conversation

Think of the funnel as a conversation with a stranger who gradually becomes a customer:

1. **Ad**: "Hey, do you have this problem? Here is something that might help." (Interrupt, qualify, intrigue.)
2. **Landing Page**: "Here is exactly what this is, who it is for, and what you get. Give me your email and I will send it to you." (Educate, build desire, capture.)
3. **Lead Magnet**: "Here is the valuable thing I promised. Notice how it gives you a quick win but leaves you wanting the full solution." (Deliver value, build trust, create gap.)
4. **Email Sequence**: "Let me tell you more about who I am, why I built this, and how I have helped people like you. When you are ready, here is the next step." (Nurture, prove, invite.)
5. **Thank-You / Confirmation Page**: "Great choice. Here is what happens next. While you are here, check this out." (Confirm, reduce buyer's remorse, upsell.)

### Maintaining Message Continuity

The reader should never feel a disconnect between what the ad promised and what the landing page delivers. These rules enforce continuity:

1. **Echo the hook**: If the ad says "5 ways to double your bookings," the landing page headline must reference doubling bookings. Do not switch metaphors or topics.
2. **Match the voice**: If the ad is casual and conversational, the landing page cannot suddenly become corporate and formal.
3. **Escalate, don't repeat**: Each step should add new information. The landing page expands on the ad's promise. The emails expand on the landing page's promise. Repeating the same words verbatim signals laziness.
4. **Consistent CTA language**: If the landing page button says "Get My Free Checklist," the ad should say "free checklist," not "free guide" or "free ebook." Inconsistent naming creates doubt.

### Landing Page Copy Structure

A standard opt-in landing page follows this sequence:

1. **Headline**: The core promise. Use one of the headline formulas above. Speaks to the desired result.
2. **Subheadline**: Adds specificity or qualifies the audience. "For [audience] who want [result] without [pain point]."
3. **Hero section supporting text (2-3 sentences)**: Expand the promise. Establish relevance. Name the pain briefly.
4. **What You Will Learn / Get (bullet list)**: 3-5 specific, benefit-driven bullets describing the lead magnet contents. Start each bullet with a verb or outcome.
5. **Social proof (optional but recommended)**: A short testimonial, a "trusted by X businesses" line, or a credibility marker.
6. **CTA**: Button with benefit-driven text. Supporting text below to reduce risk.
7. **Brief bio (optional)**: One paragraph about who is behind this. Builds trust. Relevant credentials only, no life story.

For sales pages, expand with: long-form problem agitation, detailed solution presentation, feature-benefit stacking, objection handling section, guarantee, multiple CTAs throughout, and a closing urgency section.

### Email Sequence Architecture

A standard post-opt-in nurture sequence has 5-7 emails over 7-14 days. Each email has one job.

**Email 1 (Immediate -- Delivery)**
- Subject line: Deliver on the promise. "Here is your [lead magnet name]"
- Body: Short. Deliver the asset. Set expectations for what comes next. One sentence about who you are. No hard sell.

**Email 2 (Day 1-2 -- Story)**
- Subject line: Curiosity or empathy hook.
- Body: Tell a story. Your story, a client's story, or a "what if" story. End with a soft connection to the offer.

**Email 3 (Day 3-4 -- Value)**
- Subject line: Teach something useful.
- Body: Share one actionable tip related to the lead magnet topic. Demonstrate expertise. Position the offer as the "full solution."

**Email 4 (Day 5-6 -- Social Proof)**
- Subject line: Client result or testimonial hook.
- Body: Case study or testimonial. Specific numbers and outcomes. "Here is how [person/business] achieved [result]."

**Email 5 (Day 7-8 -- Objection Handling)**
- Subject line: Address the biggest objection directly.
- Body: Name the objection. Validate it. Overcome it with evidence or logic. Soft CTA.

**Email 6 (Day 9-10 -- Direct Offer)**
- Subject line: Clear and direct.
- Body: Make the pitch. Summarize the offer, the benefits, the proof, and the guarantee. Strong CTA.

**Email 7 (Day 12-14 -- Last Chance)**
- Subject line: Urgency or "final reminder."
- Body: Brief. Recap the core benefit. Remind them of the deadline or limited availability (if real). Final CTA.

### Email Subject Line Principles

- Keep under 50 characters when possible.
- Curiosity, benefit, or urgency -- pick one per subject line.
- Personalization (first name) can help but is not required.
- Avoid spam trigger words: "free" in all caps, excessive punctuation, "act now."
- Test lowercase subject lines -- they can feel more personal.
- The subject line's only job is to get the email opened. The first line's job is to get the next line read.

### Ad Copy Structure

Ads are the top of the funnel. They must do three things in very little space: stop the scroll, qualify the reader, and compel the click.

**Short-form ad (Facebook/Instagram feed):**
- Line 1: Hook. A question, bold claim, or surprising stat. This line must stop the scroll.
- Line 2-3: Agitate the problem or tease the solution. Keep it to the reader's reality.
- Line 4: Bridge to the offer. "That is why I created [lead magnet name]."
- Line 5: CTA. "Download it free here." / "Grab your copy -- link in comments."

**Long-form ad (Facebook for cold traffic):**
- Line 1: Hook.
- Lines 2-6: Story or problem expansion. PAS framework works well here.
- Lines 7-8: Introduce the solution / lead magnet.
- Lines 9-10: Bullet the top 2-3 things they will learn or get.
- Final line: CTA with link.

**Google Search ad:**
- Headline 1: Match the search intent. Include the keyword.
- Headline 2: Benefit or differentiator.
- Headline 3: CTA or trust signal.
- Description: Expand the benefit. Include specifics. End with CTA.

### Thank-You Page Copy

The thank-you page is one of the most underused assets in a funnel. The reader just took action -- they are engaged and trusting. Use this moment.

**Structure:**
1. **Confirmation**: "You are in. Check your email for [lead magnet name]." Clear and immediate.
2. **What to expect next**: "Over the next week, I will send you [brief description of email sequence]. Keep an eye on your inbox."
3. **Next step offer (optional)**: A low-friction next step. Book a free call, join a community, watch a short video. This is not a hard sell -- it is a natural continuation.
4. **Share prompt (optional)**: "Know someone who would find this useful? Send them this link."


## Writing Process

When writing funnel copy, follow this sequence:

1. **Gather inputs**: Client brief, audience description, existing copy, testimonials, reviews, competitor examples. Extract customer language.
2. **Define the voice**: Set the three adjectives. Write the test paragraph. Confirm alignment.
3. **Map the funnel**: Determine which assets are needed (ad, landing page, lead magnet, email sequence, thank-you page). Confirm the flow.
4. **Write the landing page first**: This is the anchor. Everything else references it.
5. **Write the email sequence**: This extends the landing page's argument across multiple touchpoints.
6. **Write the ad copy**: This is the distilled version of the landing page's hook and promise.
7. **Write the thank-you page**: This bridges the opt-in to the nurture sequence.
8. **Review for continuity**: Read the entire funnel in sequence. Check that the hook, voice, offer language, and CTA are consistent across every piece.
9. **Edit for brevity**: Cut every sentence that does not earn its place. Shorten paragraphs. Remove filler words.
