# Content Adaptation Skill

This skill provides the methodology for breaking source content into repurposable elements and reassembling them into platform-native content. The goal is not copy-paste reformatting -- it's intelligent adaptation that extracts the BEST elements and reshapes them for each platform's unique strengths.

## Core Principle

One piece of content contains multiple "content atoms" -- ideas, stories, data points, quotes, frameworks, and arguments that can each become a standalone piece on a different platform. The art of repurposing is identifying these atoms, selecting the right ones for each platform, and reshaping them to fit.

Bad repurposing: Taking a blog post and splitting it into random paragraphs for Twitter.
Good repurposing: Extracting the most contrarian insight from a blog post and framing it as a Twitter hook, then building a new argument around it using thread-native storytelling.

## Content Atom Extraction

### What Qualifies as a Content Atom

A content atom is any element from the source that can stand alone AND would stop someone from scrolling on at least one platform.

| Atom Type | Description | Best Platform Fit |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Hot Take** | A strong opinion or contrarian view | X thread, LinkedIn, video script |
| **Framework** | A numbered method, process, or mental model | Instagram carousel, blog post, X thread |
| **Story** | A personal anecdote with a lesson | LinkedIn, email newsletter, video script |
| **Data Point** | A statistic, number, or research finding | X tweet, pull quote, blog post |
| **Quote** | A punchy, standalone phrase | Pull quote graphic, audiogram, X tweet |
| **Example** | A case study or real-world illustration | Blog post, LinkedIn, email newsletter |
| **Tip** | A single actionable piece of advice | Video script, Instagram slide, X tweet |
| **Question** | A thought-provoking question that sparks reflection | LinkedIn post, email subject line |

### Extraction Priority

When analyzing source content, prioritize atoms in this order:
1. **Contrarian takes** -- these drive the most engagement everywhere
2. **Personal stories** -- these build connection and are hardest to replicate
3. **Specific data points** -- these add credibility and are shareable
4. **Frameworks/processes** -- these provide value and get saved/bookmarked
5. **Memorable quotes** -- these travel on their own
6. **Practical tips** -- these round out the package

## The Adaptation Matrix

For each content atom, the adaptation approach differs by platform:

### Short-Form Platforms (X, Instagram, Video Scripts)

**Compression rules**:
- One idea per piece. No tangents.
- Lead with the punchline, not the setup.
- Remove qualifying language ("I think", "maybe", "in some cases")
- Use the strongest version of the claim.
- Cut context that the platform audience doesn't need.

**What to add**:
- Platform-native hooks (scroll-stopping first lines)
- Visual direction for Instagram and video
- Engagement prompts (questions, CTAs)

### Medium-Form Platforms (LinkedIn, Email Newsletter)

**Adaptation rules**:
- Maintain nuance but tighten structure
- Can include a brief story or example for context
- Should feel like a complete thought, not an excerpt
- Personal perspective matters more than pure information

**What to add**:
- Professional or personal framing (LinkedIn vs. email)
- Contextual setup (1-2 sentences) that the atom doesn't naturally have
- A "so what" conclusion that ties to the reader's life

### Long-Form Platforms (Blog Post)

**Expansion rules**:
- Multiple atoms combine into one piece
- Add SEO structure (H2s, target keywords, meta description)
- Expand examples, add context, include supporting evidence
- Build a complete argument arc (intro > sections > conclusion)

**What to add**:
- SEO metadata
- Internal/external link suggestions
- Image placement suggestions
- Comprehensive coverage of the topic (not just the atom)

## Repurposing Anti-Patterns

### 1. The "Copy-Paste Split"
**What it is**: Taking a blog post and breaking it into sequential paragraphs for a Twitter thread.
**Why it fails**: Blog paragraphs aren't tweets. They have different hooks, different pacing, different conclusions.
**Fix**: Rewrite from scratch using the content atoms. The thread should feel native to Twitter.

### 2. The "Everything Everywhere"
**What it is**: Using the same talking points on every single platform.
**Why it fails**: Audiences overlap. Followers on multiple platforms see the same content repeated.
**Fix**: Each platform gets a DIFFERENT atom or a different ANGLE on the same atom.

### 3. The "Headline Swap"
**What it is**: Writing one piece and changing only the headline/formatting for each platform.
**Why it fails**: Platform conventions are radically different. What works on LinkedIn fails on X.
**Fix**: Rewrite for each platform. The underlying insight can be the same, but the execution must be native.

### 4. The "Context Collapse"
**What it is**: Pulling a quote or point out of context so it means something different than intended.
**Why it fails**: Misrepresentation damages trust and credibility.
**Fix**: Only extract atoms that work standalone. If an atom needs context, provide it or skip it.

### 5. The "Overstuff"
**What it is**: Trying to fit 3 ideas into a single short-form piece.
**Why it fails**: Short-form content only has room for one idea.
**Fix**: One atom per short-form piece. Period.

## Angle Strategy

The same content atom can be presented with different "angles" to make each platform piece feel fresh:

### Angle Types

1. **The Lesson**: "Here's what I learned from [experience]"
2. **The Framework**: "[Number] steps to [outcome]"
3. **The Contrarian**: "Everyone says X. Here's why they're wrong."
4. **The Story**: "Last week, [personal anecdote]. Here's the takeaway."
5. **The Challenge**: "Most people [common mistake]. Are you?"
6. **The Data**: "[Surprising stat]. Here's what it means."
7. **The How-To**: "How to [do thing] in [timeframe/steps]"
8. **The Why**: "Why [thing] matters more than you think"

### Angle Assignment by Platform

| Platform | Best Angles | Why |
|----------|-------------|-----|
| X/Twitter | Contrarian, Framework, Challenge | Twitter rewards boldness and structure |
| LinkedIn | Lesson, Story, Why | LinkedIn rewards reflection and narrative |
| Instagram | Framework, How-To, Challenge | Instagram rewards visual, actionable content |
| Email | Story, Lesson, Data | Email rewards personal connection and depth |
| Blog | How-To, Framework, Why | Blog rewards comprehensiveness and SEO |
| Video | Contrarian, Challenge, Story | Video rewards emotion and energy |

## Content Freshness Check

Before finalizing a repurposed piece, verify:

1. **Standalone test**: Can someone who never saw the source content understand and enjoy this?
2. **Platform native test**: Would a platform-native creator produce something that looks like this?
3. **Value test**: Does this provide value on its own (not just a teaser for the original)?
4. **Audience test**: Is this appropriate for the platform's audience and culture?
5. **Freshness test**: If a follower saw both the original AND this repurposed piece, would they feel like they got new value (not just a repeat)?

## Source Content Type Handling

### Podcast/Video Transcripts
- Transcripts are messy. Expect filler words, incomplete thoughts, tangents.
- The best content atoms often come from tangents (the creator's natural passion shows).
- Remove verbal tics in repurposed content, but keep the energy and personality.
- Timestamps are valuable -- note them for audiogram selection.

### Blog Posts/Articles
- Already structured. The extraction is more straightforward.
- Look for points that are buried in paragraphs but deserve spotlight treatment.
- The conclusion often contains the strongest standalone insights.

### Social Media Posts
- Already short-form. The atom IS the content.
- Repurposing is about expanding and adapting to other platforms.
- Look for what's implied but not stated (the reader fills in gaps that a newsletter reader needs spelled out).

### Newsletter Issues
- Mix of curated and original content. Focus on the original commentary.
- The personal voice is strongest here -- use it as a voice calibration source.
- Links and references can be expanded into standalone blog posts.
