Behavioral Influence

What influences behavior?
At a macro level:
Predominately all behavior is driven by survival (avoiding pain) and reproduction (seeking pleasure). 
Humans are self-serving first & foremost.
Humans will embody other characteristics and their behavior driven by other things, but that very quickly changes in life or death situations, where you see human nature in its rawest and most unfiltered form.
Survival and reproduction go hand-in-hand. You can’t reproduce if you aren’t alive.
The ancestral environment (that for thousands of years, engineered who we are today) consisted of limited resources (food, shelter, mates) which gave birth to fierce competition, so self-interest came naturally. 
At a micro level:
External Stimuli(s)/Trigger(s)
Something that stimulates and/or prompts a tendency to act or react that is external.
Cognitive Heuristic(s) and/or Cognitive Bias
Pre-programmed mental shortcuts that process incoming stimuli/information to facilitate quick (often, irrational) actions.
Ability
The assessment of easy or difficult completing the required action is going to be.
Motivation
How strong the inclination to act is.
Pain/Pleasure = Immediate
Hope/Fear = Anticipation
Social Acceptance/Rejection = Last
 = Behavioral Reaction

The 7 Overarching Principles Of Psychology 
Reciprocation
People are wired to want (even need) to return favors and pay back debt
We are social animals that rely on others for survival.
Commitment
We have a deep need to be seen as consistent, predominately with our self-image.
Who we say we are and who tell other people we are, has a very strong pull on our behavior.
Social Proof
We feel and seek validation based on what others are doing.
Safety in numbers
Authority
We are programmed to respect, obey, and listen to authority figures, from a very young age.
Liking
The more we know, like, and trust someone, the more likely we are to comply with their requests.
Even displaying and mirroring similarity, will subconsciously allow you to have more influence over someone's behavior.
Scarcity
We have a tendency to overvalue things that are of limited supply.
The less there is, the more valuable it is.
Unity
We are strongly influenced by shared identities
The tribal mentality

Heuristics & Biases 
Association
People automatically connect the pain or pleasure of something new, to something old(er).
Rewards & Punishment
People act in accordance with incentives.
Reality Distortion
People will distort and deceive themselves in order to reduce pain or at times, enhance pleasure.
Confirmation Bias
People will actively seek out information that confirms their already existing beliefs/values and avoid information that contradicts them.
Cognitive Dissonance
People will change their beliefs, values, and attitudes when their actions contradict those same beliefs, values, and attitudes
Status Quo Bias
People have a tendency to like things just the way they are, and they don’t like change.
Anchoring
People have a tendency to overweigh the importance of initial information as a reference point for decisions (price anchoring)
Sensemaking
People have a tendency to construct explanations to fit an often irrational desire or action.
Reasoning Bias
People are more likely to comply with requests when there is a reason associated with them, regardless of what the reason is.
Emotional State
People will act according to their pre-existing emotional state
Reflection Effect
People have a tendency to be risk-averse when there is something to gain, and risk-taking when there is something to lose.
Perceptual Incongruence
People pay attention to things they did not expect and therefore seek more information to understand.
Availability Heuristic
People are more likely to believe an outcome can occur based on how easily they can imagine it.

Part 2 - Nailing Your ICP & TAMMost advertisers don’t fail because of bad campaign set-up or ads, they fail with ads because of bad messaging alignment or a non-viable TAM.
If your offer, message, and audience are out of sync.If you’re speaking to the wrong people, in the wrong way, at the wrong stage of awareness.
You won’t get the results you’re seeking.
This section aims to fix that. By the end, you’ll know:
Who you’re actually talking to (your ICP)
How big your true opportunity is (your TAM)
And how to adjust your message to match your market’s awareness and sophistication.
1. Understanding TAM & ICP
Let’s start with clear definitions:
Total Addressable Market (TAM)
Your TAM is the total size of the problem you solve, the complete universe of people or businesses who could theoretically buy your offer.
It answers the question:
“If everyone who could benefit from my solution actually bought it, how big would that market be?”
Example:
 My TAM isn’t just agency owners who want more clients.
It’s every info-product business or service provider running paid ads who struggles with acquisition or scaling profitably.
So even though your ideal client might be a 7-figure offer owner, my TAM includes 6-figure coaches, consultants, and hybrid creators, anyone in that ecosystem with the same underlying problem.
Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
Your ICP isn’t who you can sell to… it’s who you should sell to.
They have the problem now
They can afford to solve it
They are ready to act
They fit your delivery model
Think of TAM as the ocean, and ICP as the species you’re fishing for.
For example:
“Info-product businesses doing $20–80K/month, running paid ads, who struggle to scale client acquisition profitably without losing margins.”

The Two Psychological Maps: Awareness & Sophistication
Once you know who you’re speaking to, the next question is how aware and sophisticated they are.These two frameworks, from Eugene Schwartz’s book Breakthrough Advertising, determine the language, angle, and claims you should use in your messaging.
A. Stages of Awareness
Every market moves through five awareness levels.Each one requires a different marketing approach.



Stage
	Description
	Message Example

1. Unaware
	Doesn’t know they have a problem.
	“Most men drift through life without a clear mission. Here’s why that kills their confidence.”

2. Problem Aware
	Feels the pain but doesn’t know the solution.
	“If you’ve tried dieting but can’t lose weight, it’s not your willpower, it’s your hormones.”

3. Solution Aware
	Knows solutions exist but not yours.
	“Most agencies focus on ad hacks. We focus on the system that makes scaling predictable.”

4. Product Aware
	Knows your offer or similar ones exist.
	“Trusted by 10,000+ women who ditched harsh skincare routines for results that last.”

5. Most Aware
	Fully understands your offer, just needs a reason to act now.
	“The doors close tonight, lock in lifetime access before prices increase.”
Your job is to identify where your ICP currently lives, and write messaging that meets them there. It might be that there are portions of the market that span different awareness stages so script ads for different awareness stages.
B. Levels of Market Sophistication
The more a market matures, the more promises it’s already seen.Each level forces you to change your message.
Level
	Market Sophistication
	Example in Context

1. First to market
	You’re the first to introduce the idea, simple, bold promise works.
	“Get clients with paid ads.”

2. Competition appears
	You must prove superiority.
	“Get more clients with an AI-driven ad system.”

3. Saturated market
	Everyone’s making similar claims, you need mechanism clarity.
	“The 3-layer Client Acquisition OS that doubles booked calls in 30 days.”

4. Jaded market
	Market is skeptical, show proof, social validation, unique process.
	“Over 200 agencies scaled using this plug-and-play acquisition engine.”

5. Hyper-sophisticated
	The market has seen everything, use identity, philosophy, or worldview.
	“Built by agency operators, for agency operators.”

2. Translating Market Insight Into Messaging, Offers & Ads
By now, you’ve:
Defined your TAM - the total universe of people who could benefit from your solution.
Defined your ICP - the precise subset of that market who is ready, able, and willing to buy.
Mapped your market’s awareness and sophistication - the two forces that shape how your message must sound.
Now, it’s time to take those insights and turn them into marketing direction, clear, concrete positioning, messaging, and creative angles that actually drive conversions.
1. How Your ICP Dictates Your Message
The goal of this section is to make your ICP feel seen.That happens when you can:
Mirror their language
Address their reality
Reframe their pain into possibility
This is where most advertisers fall short, they build creative from their point of view, not the customer’s.
So we’ll use your ICP to build your “Messaging Triangle”, a simple 3-part model you can use for any ad or funnel.
The Messaging Triangle
At the core of every effective message are three components:
Corner
	Definition
	Example (Ad Consulting Offer)

Pain / Problem
	The frustration your ICP is aware of
	“You’re spending more on ads but calls aren’t increasing.”

Desire / Aspiration
	The outcome they crave but can’t reach
	“You want predictable, scalable acquisition, without burning cash.”

Mechanism / Differentiator
	The reason your solution works when others fail
	“Our 3-phase system pinpoints weak conversion points across your funnel.”
Together, these three corners give your ad narrative direction and emotional weight.Your ICP research tells you what language to use at each corner.

Part 3 - Simplifying the next steps


Before writing copy you should ask the following questions.Over time they will become unconscious but in the future (and in the following modules) you should refer back to these 2 list to ensure you are going in the right direction with your copy

I’ve provided a simplified list and a far more extensive list of questions you should consider

Simplified List:
Who is the product for?
How does it work?
How can they know it works?
How can they know it will work for them?

Extended List:
What does he know about you?
If he knows nothing about us, then we know we have sell ourselves just as much as the solution (est. trust/authority)
If he already knows and trust us, then we need not waste time building trust or establishing a relationship (warm traffic vs. cold traffic)
What does he know about your product?
If he knows nothing about our product, then the length we’ll have to go to sell it is far, far greater.
If he knows about our product and believes it will work for him, then we may simply need to present the product in front of him, potentially with an incentive to move forward now.
What does he believe about himself?
If he believes he is an action-taker, we may simply just remind him of that alongside the cost of inaction
If he believes he will never succeed, we may showcase the scary cost of that belief long-term in an attempt to change it.
What does he know about his own problems?
If he knows that this problem has been lingering a long time, behind multiple attempts to solve it… we may need to illustrate why everything before has let him down.
If he knows (or believes) certain problems will be solved when he reaches a certain state (or outcome) then we simply have to connect our product/solution to that outcome.
What does he know about other solutions?
How easy is the product to understand?
Will an enlarged promise be easily accepted?
Have you got an exceptionally good deal or guarantee to offer?
Does your customer know, trust, and deal with you often?
Have you got a superior product that the market is “expecting?”
Will your prospect have any reason to believe anything you say?
Will your prospect even accept that any solution to his problem is possible?
Does your prospect even know that there’s a problem worth solving?
Do your claims sound similar to everyone else's?

There are key elements of any marketing messaging, before creating your next ad, confirm the following:

Audience Clarity → You know exactly who this ad is for.
Stage of Awareness → You’ve identified where they are psychologically.
Market Sophistication → You know what claims or angles are already saturated. So you can say something different.
Emotion & Outcome → You’ve chosen the emotional tone and end goal of the ad.
Message-Market Match → Your hook and offer align with that awareness stage.
Proof Elements → You can show, not tell (case studies, testimonials, results).
Action Clarity → You’ve defined what happens after they click, the next step in your funnel.

Closing Thought

Most advertisers overcomplicate this process.
They get lost in tactics, budgets, placements, campaign structures, and forget that ads are simply human conversations at scale.

When you focus on clarity, alignment, and emotional truth, everything else compounds: the algorithm favors you, your audience trusts you, and your creative performs better across every format.

So as you move into the next modules, where we’ll cover ad creation, landing page copy, and VSL development, keep one idea in mind:

Simplicity wins.

Not because it’s easier, but because it’s clearer, faster, and truer to what people actually respond to.
