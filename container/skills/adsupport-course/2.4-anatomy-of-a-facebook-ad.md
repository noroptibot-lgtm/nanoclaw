3. The Conversion Heuristic

The conversion heuristic created by MECLABS I believe and adapted by others is a way to analyse ads and funnel to determine where they might be falling short.The elements here are 
C = 4M + 3V + 2(I - F) - 2A
Where:
C = Probability of Conversion
M = Motivation of the user
How motivated they are before arriving on the page
Often driven by source/traffic quality
High motivation = easier to convert
Low motivation = harder to convert
You can’t change it easily; you can only align with it
V = Value Proposition
Value is always determined from the perspective of your ideal future customer
Sub-components of Value
Appeal (How much do I want this?)
Exclusivity (Where else can I get this?)
Clarity (How well do I understand this?)
Do they clearly understand what they get?
Articulation of benefit
Why this? Why you? Why now?
Incentive (What else is in it for me?)
I = Incentive
Anything that increases desire to take the action now
Bonuses
Guarantees
Urgency
Scarcity
Free training / lead magnet payoff
“Stacking the value”
F = Friction
Can also be called perceived ‘Cost’
Mental Cost (How difficult will this be)
Fiscal Cost (How much money must I exchange for this?)
Time Cost (How long will this take?)
Examples that increase friction/cost
Too many fields on a form
Confusing page layout
Hard-to-watch VSL
Long walls of text
Slow page load times
A = Anxiety
Fear, doubt, uncertainty
Credibility (How much do I believe you?)
“Is this legit?”
“Will this work for me?”
“What happens when I click?”
Solved by:
Testimonials
Action Clarity
Social proof
Risk reversal
What that actually means for your ads
When crafting ads, landing pages, and VSLs, you’re always trying to:
Increase Motivation (M) → Target the right ICP, speak directly to their pains/desires.
Maximize Value (V) → Sharp/clear positioning, clear promise/outcome.
Add Incentive (I) → Give the user a reason to act now (training payoff, scarcity, bonuses).
Reduce Friction (F) → Shorter forms, simpler pages, easier consumption.
Reduce Anxiety (A) → Trust assets, testimonials, transparency, action clarity.

M = Motivation of the User
How motivated they are before arriving on the page. There is debate about whether motivation can be created or is inherent. We will assume it can’t be created for this training and that it will be defined by the audience you target and speak to with your ad.
High-Motivation Examples (Good Traffic Source)
Someone actively searching for “how to get more coaching clients” 
A warm lead who has consumed your content for weeks, now seeing an ad to your VSL.
A prospect who is already problem-aware: “My agency is stuck at $10k/month and I can’t scale due to client churn”
Vs. “I’d like to get more clients”
Low-Motivation Examples (Cold Traffic)
Broad audience ad to people with no stated interest in business, coaching or marketing.
Someone scrolling memes at 11PM, not thinking about their business at all.
A person who doesn’t yet believe they have a problem: “My coaching business is fine right now, I don’t need help.”
How this shows up in a Facebook ad:
High motivation → shorter copy works, direct promise lands strongly.
Low motivation → you need more education, curiosity, or emotional narrative to “open the loop.”
V = Value Proposition (Inside the Ad Itself)
Value is always determined from the perspective of the future customer.
Appeal (How much do I want this?)
High Appeal Example:
“Scale your coaching business to consistent $20k-$50k months without relying on guesswork or low-quality leads.”
Low Appeal Example:
“Join my newsletter for weekly marketing tips.” (No urgent gain, no transformation.)
Exclusivity (Where else can I get this?)
High Exclusivity Example:
“The exact client acquisition system used by top 1% coaches and agency owners to scale predictably in 2026”
Low Exclusivity Example:
“We teach you how to run facebook ads profitably.”
Clarity (Do I clearly understand the outcome?)
High Clarity Example:
“Watch the free 17-minute training that shows the 3-part system we use to help coaches generate 5-15 qualified calls/week.”
Low Clarity Example:
“Unlock your true potential with this method.”  (No specifics = low clarity.)
Why This? Why You? Why Now?
High Value Example:
“After $30M+ managed in ad spend, we’ve documented the exact funnel structure working right now in 2025.”
Low Value Example:
“We’ve helped 100’s of clients scale their ads” (Weak, unspecific, no proof.)
Incentive (What else is in it for me?)
High Incentive Example:
“Get the free ‘Client Acquisition Map’ PDF immediately after watching the training.”
Low Incentive Example:
“Click to learn more.” (No additional benefit.)
I = Incentive (Action Right Now)
Anything that increases desire to take action now.
Facebook Ad Incentive Examples
“Free workbook included when you sign up.”
“Limited-time access to the updated 2025 funnel playbook.”
“Join 9,800+ coaches using this method.”
“Watch the training and get our free DM sorcery template.”
F = Friction (Cost) inside the Facebook Ad → Click → Funnel Flow
Mental, time and fiscal cost.
Examples of Friction in a Facebook Ad
Copy that’s too complex or uses jargon that is hard to understand.
A video ad that takes 40 seconds to get to the point.
A headline like:
 “Apply for 90-day coaching program” (Heavy mental load.)
Reducing friction
“Get 6-pack abs in just 5-minutes per day”(reduces perceived time/effort commitment)
Examples of Friction After Clicking
High friction examples:
Asking for name, email, phone, business name, website, and revenue range right on the opt-in.
A VSL that is 27 minutes long with no preview or promise.
A slow-loading mobile page (major performance killer).
A page with mismatched messaging from the ad.
Low friction examples:
“Just enter your email to watch the free training.”
Page loads fast, simple headline + video.
VSL has a clear promise and payoff.
A = Anxiety (Trust Issues That Kill Ad Performance)
Fear, doubt, uncertainty.
Examples of Anxiety Triggered by Poor Facebook Ads
“Is this legit?” → No social proof, no credibility, generic claims.
“Is this a scam?” → Overhyped promises with no evidence.
“Will this work for me?” → No testimonials that reflect the viewer’s demographic.
“What happens when I click?” → Ambiguous CTA like “Learn More” with no explanation.
Examples of Reducing Anxiety in Facebook Ads (Good)
“Join 3,200+ coaches who’ve gone through this training.”
“Backed by real results from 7-figure agencies and coaches.”
“Watch the free training instantly, no pitch, no credit card.”
“Rated 5* on trust pilot” (show in ad)
Ad showing your face → Builds trust.
4. Writing Your Video Ad Scripts
Before you get started on writing your video ad scripts, pull up your finished VSL document as well and your landing page copy.

It’s key you match your ads with what they will ultimately see next. Also the key elements of your VSL should be used in a condensed form in your ads. Often I see people say the right things but wait too long to say them.

I’d recommend you script at least 10 ads and pick the best 5 when it comes to launching.

There are lots of different ways to script ads and I used to advocate for the harmon brothers modular formula (Hook → Body → CTA) but given Meta’s recent Andromeda update and number of other factors I recommend scripting individual ads . I’ll show you what I mean.

Now we need to handle the ad copy which will appear alongside your image or video. This should be pretty simple now you have your ads scripted.
