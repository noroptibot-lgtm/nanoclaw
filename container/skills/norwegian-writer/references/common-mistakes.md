# Common Norwegian mistakes

## Å vs og

- **å** = "to" (before infinitive). "Jeg liker å lese."
- **og** = "and". "Jeg liker bøker og filmer."

Test: replace with English. If "to" works, use "å". If "and" works, use "og".

Wrong: "Jeg prøver og lære." Right: "Jeg prøver å lære."
Wrong: "Lese å skrive." Right: "Lese og skrive."

## Da vs når

- **da** = one specific time in the past. "Da jeg var liten..."
- **når** = repeated, future, or general. "Når jeg drikker kaffe, blir jeg våken."

Test: "every time" → når. "that one time" → da.

## Enda vs ennå

- **enda** = even more, still (comparative). "Enda bedre." "Enda en gang."
- **ennå** = yet, up until now (time). "Han har ikke kommet ennå."

## De / dem / deres

- **de** = they (subject). "De kommer."
- **dem** = them (object). "Jeg så dem."
- **deres** = their/theirs. "Bilen deres."

Test: subject or object? "They" → de. "Them" → dem.

## Sin / sitt / sine vs hans / hennes / deres

Reflexive (sin/sitt/sine) refers to the **subject of the same clause**.

- "Per tok sin bil." = Per took his own car.
- "Per tok hans bil." = Per took someone else's car.
- "Kari elsker mannen sin." = Kari loves her own husband.
- "Kari elsker mannen hennes." = Kari loves another woman's husband.

Get this wrong and natives wince.

## Definite forms

Norwegian double-marks definiteness, but not always.

- "bilen" = the car ✓
- "den røde bilen" = the red car ✓ (adjective triggers double)
- "den bilen" = that car (demonstrative, points)
- "den bil" = wrong, never write this

Bare definite ("bilen") is the default. Double ("den ... -en") only with adjective or pointing.

## Time prepositions (English speakers fail this)

- "I have lived here for 5 years" → "Jeg har bodd her i 5 år." (NOT "for 5 år")
- "I will be there in 5 minutes" → "Jeg er der om 5 minutter." (NOT "i 5 minutter")
- "On Monday" → "På mandag" (future) or "mandag" (general)
- "In the morning" → "Om morgenen"
- "At 3 PM" → "klokka 15" or "kl. 15.00"

## Anglicisms to avoid

| Anglicism (avoid) | Native Norwegian |
|---|---|
| å sjekke ut | å se på, å undersøke |
| å booke | å bestille |
| å sende en email | å sende en e-post |
| en deal | en avtale |
| feedback | tilbakemelding |
| å pushe | å presse, å dytte |
| å performe | å prestere, å yte |
| business | bedrift, virksomhet |
| en meeting | et møte |
| en update | en oppdatering |

Loanwords like "marketing", "lead", "pixel", "tracking" are OK in marketing-internal Norwegian, but always use Norwegian words for customer-facing copy.

## False friends

- **eventuelt** ≠ eventually. It means "possibly" or "if applicable". "Eventually" = "til slutt".
- **aktuell** ≠ actual. It means "current" or "relevant". "Actual" = "egentlig" or "faktisk".
- **gymnas** ≠ gym. It means "high school". "Gym" = "treningsstudio".
- **fabrikk** = factory. **stoff** = fabric/material.

## Apostrophes and possessives

Norwegian possessive uses **-s** with no apostrophe.

- "Pers bil" (not "Per's bil")
- "Selskapets ansvar" (not "Selskapet's")

Apostrophe + s only for names ending in s, x, z: "Mads' bil", "Marx' teori".

## Comma rules

- Comma before "men", "for", "så", "eller" when joining two main clauses.
- Comma after a leading subordinate clause: "Når jeg kommer hjem, skal jeg lage mat."
- No Oxford comma: "epler, pærer og bananer".

## Capital letters (Norwegian uses fewer than English)

Lowercase in Norwegian:
- Days: mandag, tirsdag
- Months: januar, februar
- Languages: norsk, engelsk
- Nationalities as adjectives: norsk mat
- Job titles: daglig leder, prosjektleder

Uppercase only at sentence start and proper nouns (people, places, brands).
