# Content Strategy Frameworks Skill

This skill provides the methodology for designing a content strategy that actually drives business results. Not a "post 3 times a week on LinkedIn" plan -- a strategic framework that connects content to business goals, maps the audience journey, and creates a defensible content position.

## Core Principle

A content strategy answers three questions:
1. **Why** are we creating content? (Business goal)
2. **Who** are we creating it for? (Audience segments)
3. **What** makes our content worth consuming over the alternatives? (Content positioning)

Everything else -- platforms, formats, frequency, topics -- flows from these three answers.

## The Content Strategy Framework

### Layer 1: Business Goal Alignment

Every piece of content should trace back to a business objective:

| Business Goal | Content Approach | Success Metric |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Brand awareness | Thought leadership, hot takes, shareable frameworks | Reach, impressions, new followers |
| Lead generation | Educational content with clear CTAs, lead magnets | Email signups, DM conversations |
| Authority building | Deep-dive content, original research, case studies | Backlinks, speaking invitations, press mentions |
| Community building | Interactive content, questions, behind-the-scenes | Comments, shares, DM conversations |
| Direct sales | Problem-solution content, testimonials, demos | Click-throughs, conversion rate |

### Layer 2: Audience Journey Mapping

Map content to where the audience is in their journey:

**Awareness** (They don't know they have a problem):
- Hot takes that challenge assumptions
- Surprising data points
- "Signs you might be..." diagnostic content

**Consideration** (They know the problem, exploring solutions):
- How-to guides and frameworks
- Comparison content
- Case studies and results

**Decision** (They're choosing between options):
- Social proof and testimonials
- Behind-the-scenes of your process
- Direct offers and CTAs

**Retention** (They're a customer, keep them engaged):
- Exclusive insights
- Community content
- Advanced techniques and pro tips

### Layer 3: Content Pillar-Cluster Model

**Content Pillars** (3-5 core topics):
- Each pillar connects to the business goal
- Each pillar has enough depth for 90 days of content
- Pillars should overlap slightly (creates natural cross-linking)

**Content Clusters** (subtopics under each pillar):
- 5-10 specific topics per pillar
- Mix of evergreen (always relevant) and timely (trend-driven)
- Each cluster can generate multiple content pieces across platforms

**Example Structure**:
```
Pillar: Email Marketing
  Cluster: Subject line optimization
  Cluster: Automation sequences
  Cluster: Deliverability
  Cluster: List building
  Cluster: Email copywriting
```

### Layer 4: Content Mix Formula

Balance content types for sustained growth:

**The 70-20-10 Rule**:
- **70% Value content**: Educational, entertaining, or inspiring. No ask.
- **20% Trust content**: Social proof, behind-the-scenes, case studies. Builds credibility.
- **10% Ask content**: Direct CTAs, offers, promotions. Converts.

**The Content Type Mix**:
- **Original insights** (30%): Your unique perspective, data, or framework
- **Curated + commentary** (20%): Industry news with your take
- **Stories** (20%): Personal anecdotes, client stories, behind-the-scenes
- **Engagement** (15%): Questions, polls, discussions
- **Promotional** (15%): Offers, launches, CTAs

### Layer 5: Competitive Content Positioning

**The Content Differentiation Matrix**:

| | Same Topics | Different Topics |
|---|---|---|
| **Same Angle** | Commodity content (avoid) | Topic differentiation |
| **Different Angle** | Angle differentiation (sweet spot) | Blue ocean content |

The goal is "angle differentiation" -- cover the same topics your audience cares about, but with a unique perspective, format, or depth that competitors don't offer.

**Positioning questions**:
1. What can we say that competitors can't? (unique experience, data, access)
2. What format can we deliver that competitors won't? (depth, frequency, production quality)
3. What audience segment are competitors ignoring?
4. What contrarian position can we credibly hold?

## Competitive Analysis Framework

### What to Analyze

For each competitor (3-5 primary competitors):

1. **Platform presence**: Where are they active? Where are they absent?
2. **Content frequency**: How often do they post per platform?
3. **Content types**: What formats do they use? What's missing?
4. **Engagement patterns**: What gets the most engagement? What falls flat?
5. **Voice and tone**: How do they sound? Where's the white space?
6. **Content pillars**: What topics do they consistently cover?
7. **Gaps**: What questions are their audience asking that they're not answering?

### Competitor Content Scoring

Rate each competitor on:
- **Consistency**: Do they post regularly? (1-5)
- **Quality**: Is the content well-produced and valuable? (1-5)
- **Engagement**: Does their audience interact? (1-5)
- **Differentiation**: Is their content unique or cookie-cutter? (1-5)
- **Strategy clarity**: Is there a clear content strategy, or random posting? (1-5)

### Identifying Opportunities

Opportunities exist where:
- Competitors are inconsistent (you can win with consistency)
- Competitors are absent from a platform (you can own it)
- Competitors cover topics superficially (you can go deeper)
- Competitors all sound the same (you can differentiate with voice)
- Audience questions go unanswered (you can fill the gap)

## The 90-Day Strategy Arc

### Month 1: Foundation (Days 1-30)
- Establish content pillars and voice
- Build foundational content (pillar pages, introductory posts)
- Test platform-specific formats
- Focus on consistency over virality
- **Goal**: Establish presence and baseline engagement

### Month 2: Growth (Days 31-60)
- Double down on what worked in Month 1
- Introduce more varied content types
- Start engagement-focused content (questions, polls, discussions)
- Begin outreach and collaboration
- **Goal**: Grow audience and engagement rate

### Month 3: Conversion (Days 61-90)
- Layer in conversion-focused content
- Launch lead magnets or content upgrades
- Increase promotional content ratio
- Analyze and optimize based on 60 days of data
- **Goal**: Convert audience into leads/customers

## Content Frequency Guidelines

### By Platform

| Platform | Minimum | Optimal | Maximum |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| X/Twitter | 3/week | 1-2/day | 3-5/day |
| LinkedIn | 2/week | 3-4/week | 1/day |
| Instagram | 2/week | 3-4/week | 1/day |
| Email | 1/week | 1/week | 2/week |
| Blog | 1/month | 2-4/month | 2/week |

### Content Batching Strategy

For solopreneurs and small teams:
- **Weekly batch**: Create 1 anchor piece + 4-5 derivative pieces
- **Monthly planning**: Map the month's pillars, themes, and key dates
- **90-day calendar**: Plan at the theme level, not the post level

## Cross-Rig Integration: Content Repurposing

The content strategy should include a "Content Repurposing Recommendations" section that:
- Identifies which anchor content is best suited for multi-platform repurposing
- Marks calendar items that are repurposing candidates (e.g., long-form blog post -> X thread + LinkedIn post + email)
- References the Content Repurposing rig as the execution tool
- Includes a "repurposing workflow" recommendation in the strategy document
